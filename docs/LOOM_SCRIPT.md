## 0:00–0:35 — What intent-driven means

“I organised the workspace around what the lawyer is trying to achieve. That’s what I mean by intent-driven: ‘I’m advising a tenant considering an exit’ gives the agent a purpose for its investigation, recommended next steps and report outline. The document provides context; the objective directs the work. I kept document chat for open-ended questions, alongside Assess risk and Build report.”

Brief preset distinction, while pointing to the selector: “Assess risk prioritises exposure, conditions and next enquiries. Build report plans around an audience and deliverable. These presets change the agent’s task guidance, rather than selecting different models.”

Presenter note: workflow comparisons demonstrated different planning context and outlines; later bounded investigations completed for both presets. We have not established that every finding consistently differs. Moving between sidebar views does not itself change the agreed agent task; changing that purpose requires a new agreed brief.

Show the three landing options, upload the synthetic lease and enter a short tenant-exit objective. Show the proposed brief and explain: “The lawyer checks and agrees the direction before investigation.” Use a clearly identified prepared workspace for results if needed; do not imply the model wait was instantaneous.

## 0:35–1:10 — Evidence, recommendations and missing information

“I added findings linked to source evidence, recommended next steps and a prominent drawer for outstanding information. The aim is to help the lawyer decide what to do next, rather than just receive a summary. Clicking a citation opens and highlights its passage in the original PDF. The highlight is a separate overlay; it doesn’t rewrite the document. Source matching confirms the quotation exists, not that the legal interpretation is correct.”

Show one finding’s relevance and next step, open the outstandinginformation drawer, then follow a citation to its highlighted source. Briefly point out Document questions in navigation without switching away from the report journey.

## 1:10–2:00 — Human judgment into a deliverable

“The lawyer can bring selected findings into an editable report and add their own recommendation. The agent can propose a draft for a section, but it only replaces the text if the lawyer accepts it. I also added explicit saves, revision history and a downloadable report, so the work can become a useful deliverable.”

Choose a destination section, add a finding, point out its count and confirmation in the report tray, open the master report, and edit a section. If showing an AI draft, identify it as a proposal, inspect its statements and show acceptance or rejection. Use the report tray to save, then choose Export PDF and briefly show the downloaded report. Show revision history only if time allows. Do not claim organisation collaboration or multi-document support.

## 2:00–2:20 — A partnership that develops with the lawyer

Briefly open Settings → Professional development profile and show the demo disclosure and learning-strategy cards.

“Longer term, I’d like the workspace to develop alongside the lawyer. This Settings concept explores experience and optional development goals, supported by expert-led evaluations and consent-based, validated feedback. It’s explicitly a UI demo: the profile does not influence the agent, and CVs are neither analysed nor uploaded.”

Do not upload a real CV during the recording or imply that behavioural learning is active.
